## Purpose
Preparing for a career as a Radio Frequency (RF) Engineer requires more than completing an electrical engineering degree. You need to develop a strong understanding of electromagnetic waves, circuit design, antenna systems, and wireless communications. RF engineers help design, test, and improve technologies used in telecommunications, satellite communications, radar systems, and wireless devices. By following the best practices outlined in this graduation learning plan, you can strengthen your technical knowledge, gain hands-on experience with RF equipment, and develop the professional skills needed to successfully apply for entry-level RF engineering positions after graduation.


## Develop Technical Knowledge
RF Engineers analyze radio frequencies, electrical systems, evaluate equipment, and design new technologies within the field. These responsibilities require knowledge of but not limited to, programming and data analysis skills in Python, MATLAB. Familiarized with RF simulation tools such as ADS (Advanced Design System), AWR (Applied Wave Research) tool kit, HFSS (High-Frequency Structure Simulator), or CST (Computer Simulation Technology) [1][4].
### Why it Matters
Without the technical knowledge using these tools you will not be seen as a candidate to hiring managers. They need engineers who know enough about the tools to be able to navigate the workspace even though you are coming out of college.

### Best Practices
1.	Look for elective classes that use these tools to gain experience.

If none are available, do the following:

2.	Plan and schedule at least 2 hours a week working towards this.
3.	Create a list of the tools in order that you want to learn them in.
4.	Look up tutorials and follow them to gain beginner experience.
5. When comfortable, find projects online that use these tools [3].
6.	Do not forget to document your projects for use on your resume and LinkedIn.
7.	When you complete a project, rinse and repeat with the next tool.


## Gain Practical Expereince
Experience in general can give us insights and allow us to adjust to that new environment. RF Engineers work in a wide range of office conditions with different people from different departments. Working with different equipment, building a wide range of products.
### Why it Matters
Without practical experience we would not be able to use the equipment that a job may ask of you, you may not know how to work within teams, or how to work in a dynamic environment like the ones in industry.
### Best Practices
- Get connected to clubs that are in or related to RF engineering i.e. IEEE Bothell, Circuits Club, Grey Hat Hackers.
- Go to the workshops and learn as much as possible [5].
- Volunteer in those clubs if you can; IEEE events and get tips from people in industry.

- Work on person projects that encapsulate designing, software tools, lab equipment (VNA’s, spectrum analyzers, oscilloscopes), and hands on circuits and antennas [3][4].
  - spend at least 3 hours a week working on it.


## Applying and Outreach
To get into the job force as a RF Engineer—you need to apply for a job and for that to happen you need to know who is hiring, what they are looking for, and how you can get in.
### Why it Matters
There are so many factors involved in getting a job, especially when getting into RF engineering, that it can be overwhelming. Being prepared can help give you steps to take and feel more manageable. 
Finding the right resources and using them to help you understand this process and what works best for you will also make this entire process easier [2].
### Best Practices
-	Use your advisor, Career services, and whatever other resources you can get your firsthand that you think will help you in this process.
1.	Finding Jobs: Job fairs (Best), Career services, Online job searches with RF engineering.
      *  For Job fair; dress nice, bring resume, build and bring project portfolio.
          *  At booth; Great them, introduce yourself, show resume and ask them about what their company is about, what they do.
          * Get their contact info if you can and reach out after.
          * Ask them what they are looking for then walk them through your resume and projects
          * After you have finished with the recruiter, thank them for their time and rinse and repeat.
2. Follow career services guidance
3. Apply for online jobs; match resume to job requirements for each job and write a cover letter connecting your resume to the jobs requirements and desires.
